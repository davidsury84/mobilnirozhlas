One container, road and rail alike

ACTS (Abroll-Container-Transport-System) is a combined transport system in which the same roll-off (abroll) container travels by road on a hooklift truck and by rail on a wagon with swivel frames. The transfer is horizontal, without a crane and without a terminal — the driver manages it alone in a few minutes, anywhere beside a track with a paved surface. ELKOPLAST builds these containers as the ABR-ACTS type within the ABROLL product line. This training will teach you how the system works, how an ACTS container differs from an ordinary roll-off, how it is coded in our catalogue, and who to offer it to and how.

What ACTS is and why it came about

Before you can explain the product, you have to be able to explain the system — the customer buys the container because of the system.

The abbreviation and its origin

ACTS = Abroll-Container-Transport-System. The system was developed in Switzerland in the 1980s and spread to other European countries (Switzerland, Austria, Germany, Poland, the Czech Republic). The goal: move waste and bulk material collection off the road and onto the railway.

The basic idea

It is not the material that is transhipped but the whole container. The same body is filled at the producer, travels by road to the railway, covers the long haul by rail and reaches its destination by road (incinerator, sorting plant, steelworks…).

The key advantage

The transfer is horizontal — the container is pulled between vehicle and wagon by the hook, running on its rollers. No crane, no terminal and no second person are needed; a paved surface beside the track is enough.

ACTS is not simply a modified ABROLL. It is an intermodal transport unit — its usability depends at the same time on the container, the road carrier vehicle, the rail swivel frame, the particular railway wagon and an approved route. So the sales rep is not selling volume and sheet thickness, but verified compatibility of the whole system.
A selling truth: a customer using ACTS is not thinking about a “container” but about a logistics chain. Anyone moving large volumes over long distances (waste, scrap, timber, coal) saves money, emissions and drivers by going by rail — and the ACTS container is the ticket into that chain.
2

How the transfer works — step by step

The railway wagon carries swivel frames — 2 to 4 of them depending on the wagon type, one frame = one container. This is the sequence you have to be able to tell:

1

Positioning

The wagon with swivel frames stands on the track, with a paved surface alongside. The road vehicle with its hooklift (abroll) gear pulls up along the wagon.

2

Releasing and swinging out the frame

The operator releases the swivel frame and swings it out to the side (roughly 30–45°) towards the vehicle. The frame forms a “bridge” between wagon and truck.

3

Hooking up

The driver reverses and the hook engages the container's hook eye (height 1570 mm — hence the NH1570 pull-on code).

4

Pulling across on the rollers

The hydraulics pull the container from the frame onto the vehicle (or the other way round). The container runs on its rear rollers and along the guides of the frame.

5

Swinging back and locking

The empty (or newly loaded) frame is returned to the longitudinal position and secured by the lock — the wagon must not depart unlocked.

6

Departure

The whole exchange of one container takes a matter of minutes and the driver manages it alone. A train can serve several points along the route (a “travelling transfer terminal”).

Remember: no crane, no forklift, no spreaders. A swivel frame + hooklift gear + rollers = the entire “technology” of the transfer. That is exactly why the system is so cheap on infrastructure.

Requirements for a transfer point (the ACTS AG / SBB benchmark)

  • A straight and level track.
  • A paved area of roughly 25 × 15 m per wagon, ideally at the same level as the rail head.
  • A minimum distance of 5 m from the main track and 4.5 m from other tracks.
  • During handling the wagon must be braked, the regime for adjacent tracks set, and where applicable the overhead line switched off and earthed.

This is a Swiss operating benchmark, not an automatically valid European standard — every specific site has to be confirmed by the infrastructure manager.

Vehicles and carrier gear in practice

According to the operator ACTS AG, ordinary four- or five-axle vehicles with hooklift or chain gear can be used — for the transfer onto the frame itself, the chain system has proved the better of the two in practice (compare them in the Videos lesson).

So the brief always has to say what gear the customer actually has — hook and chain are both possible, but container and carrier must be a proven pair.

How an ACTS container differs from an ordinary roll-off

At first glance it looks like any other roll-off container. The differences are in the details — and those are exactly what the customer's engineers ask about.

What it shares with a roll-off

  • A hook eye for the pull-on hook — height 1570 mm; the standard published ELKOPLAST version has a folding hook NS 1570/50 (folding it down avoids fouling the railway loading gauge).
  • Rear rollers on which it runs while being pulled on and pushed off.
  • Tipping and emptying with the same equipment as an ordinary roll-off (rear doors, a tailgate flap, a folding front…).

What the railway demands on top

  • A reinforced frame and floor — the shocks and vibration on the railway (shunting, starting) are harsher than on the road.
  • Dimensional and geometric compatibility with the swivel frame — the container must sit exactly in the frame's guides and locking elements.
  • Securing against shifting and bouncing on the frame (the lock) throughout the train journey.
  • The loading gauge of the line: a width of up to about 2.5 m and a limited height — which is why overall height is watched more closely with ACTS than with a road roll-off.

Lengths

Typically about 5–7 m depending on the type and the order (the code always carries the exact size, e.g. 6500x2300x1500).

Volumes

Of the order of 10–38 m³ depending on the side height — from low ones for scrap to tall ones for light waste and wood chips.

Equipment

Rear doors or a tailgate flap, a tarpaulin, a net, lids or a hydraulic roof — depending on the material carried.

Material

Structural steel; for abrasive materials (scrap, rubble) wear-resistant Hardox-type plate on the exposed surfaces.

Wagons, frames and weight limits

Maximum gross weight is not a universal property of “an ACTS container”. What always applies is the lowest limit anywhere in the chain.

The decisive weight rule

max. payload = min（limit of the container, the wagon, the swivel frame, the road combination, the approved route）− tare

The road limit of the combination differs from the rail limit of the wagon, and that differs again from the capacity of the frame. A quotation may promise only the lowest of those figures — and only after the whole route has been verified.

So the same container may carry almost twice as much on one wagon as on another. A newer GATX solution (2026) uses four swivel frames for 5.95 m containers, or hybrid 20' ACTS/ISO units (both horizontal and crane handling) with a maximum of 20 t per container — the hybrid is an interesting commercial direction.

Volume × density — always do the sum

30 m³ of wood chips at 0.30 t/m³ ≈ 9 t — fine. The same container full of material with a density of 1.70 t/m³ would weigh 51 t — completely unrealistic for ACTS. Never propose a volume without the real bulk density; with dense materials you hit the weight limit long before the container is full.

The market's dimensional benchmark

European competitors (Werner & Weber) offer an ACTS range of roughly 13–31 m³: length ~6.1 m, width 2.50–2.55 m, height 1.27–2.63 m, tare 2.95–5.1 t; the operator ACTS AG also uses special 40 m³ containers for very light materials. Treat this as market orientation — ELKOPLAST parameters always come from the technical specification for the order.

Coding ACTS in the ELKOPLAST catalogue

ACTS is one of the types in the ABROLL line. The product name is a kit of parts — type, dimensions, pull-on gear, equipment.

How to read the code

The base is the type ABR-ACTS (“Rail container / ACTS container” from page 3 of the ABROLL coding table). It is followed by the same blocks as with the other roll-offs:

ABR-ACTS → 6500x2300x1500-53 (l×w×h and volume) → NH1570_60 (pull-on gear, 1570 mm eye) → floor/sides → doors or flap (e.g. KLS) → rollers/pin → roof or tarpaulin (e.g. SHP = hydraulic roof, hinges on the right).

You will practise the coding in detail in the “ABROLL — coding and configuration” training. Here it is enough to know that the ACTS type is the base of the code and decides rail compatibility; the rest of the kit is identical to the other roll-offs.

A real order

ABR-ACTS-WERNER — a customer-specific ACTS version for a particular buyer. A customer suffix never replaces the technical brief.

A real order

ABR-ACTS + hydraulic roof — on the railway, covering the load against wind-blown loss is a frequent requirement; a hydraulic roof handles it without sheeting by hand.

A real order

ABR-ACTS-KLS — the version with a rear tailgate flap (code KLS) for easy discharge of bulk materials.

Do not mix them up: ABR-DSD = road container to DIN 30722-1, ABR-DSD2 = DIN 30722-2, ABR-AFS = the French AFNOR standard, ABR-ACTS = the ACTS rail system. The wrong type at the start of the code invalidates the whole configuration.

What travels in ACTS — choosing by material

Look for large, repeating volumes of bulk or loose material over longer distances — that is where the railway wins.

Municipal and sorted waste

Collection companies and towns: hauling waste to incinerators (energy-from-waste) and sorting plants. The classic ACTS application — large volumes, a regular service.

Scrap metal

Scrapyards and steelworks: a heavy abrasive material, lower sides, wear-resistant plate. The railway handles weights that the road carries inefficiently.

Timber, wood chips, biomass

Sawmills, heating plants, biomass power stations: a light bulky material → tall containers, a net or tarpaulin against wind-blown loss.

Coal and bulk raw materials

Supplying heating plants and industry; discharge through the flap (KLS) or the doors. Dusty materials → covering (lids, a roof).

Construction rubble and soil

Large building and reclamation sites: short road runs around the station plus the long haul by rail.

Sludge and specific wastes

Wastewater treatment plants and industry: closed versions with lids or a roof; with special wastes always check the transport legislation.

Standards, approvals and ADR/RID

This is where it is easiest to make the mistake that lands the customer in trouble. Never promise a certification you do not have in black and white.

The DIN 30722 series (updated August 2025)

  • DIN 30722-1 — system 1570, vehicles up to 26 t
  • DIN 30722-2 — system 1570, vehicles up to 40 t
  • DIN 30722-3 — system 900, vehicles up to 12 t

The scope of the standard is not automatically the permitted gross weight of a particular container — that is set by the chain from lesson 4.

The withdrawn DIN 30722-4

The former DIN 30722-4:1994 for road and rail containers has been withdrawn since 2007. It must not be presented in a quotation as a valid certification — rail fitness is demonstrated differently (see below).

What has to be dealt with for rail operation

Design approval for the particular transfer system · compatibility with the swivel frame and the wagon · the railway loading gauge · coding of the loading unit · identification and code plates · tests of the structure and of the securing · the rules of the particular railway undertaking.

The UIC handbook cites approval under UIC 591 and marking under UIC 596-6 for horizontally transferred swap units — but some of the older UIC leaflets have been replaced by newer rules or contractual procedures, so always verify the current requirements with the carrier. Non-ISO intermodal units are identified by an ILU code under EN 13044-1: 4 owner letters + 6 digits + a check digit.

ADR/RID: the designation “ACTS” on its own does not mean approval for dangerous goods. For goods in packages, what is assessed is the packaging, the securing, the marking and the transport regime; BK1/BK2 concerns solid dangerous substances carried in bulk — BK2 (a closed container) applies only where that method is permitted for the particular UN number and the container meets the design and approval requirements. On the railway, the corresponding RID applies alongside ADR; what counts is the current ADR 2025 / RID 2025.

The sales argument — why ACTS

You are always comparing against two alternatives: pure road collection, and classic crane transhipment (large ISO / ABROLL containers lifted by crane).

“We have neither a terminal nor a crane.”

That is exactly why ACTS exists. A track with a paved area is enough — the swivel frame on the wagon and the hooklift gear do the rest. Zero investment in transfer equipment.

“Rail is slow and inflexible.”

The train covers the long haul, the truck the short ends — and because it is the same container, nobody ever waits for the material to be transferred. Exchanging a container on the frame takes minutes.

“We already have roll-off containers, we'll use those.”

An ordinary road roll-off does not belong on a swivel frame — it has neither the railway reinforcement nor guaranteed compatibility with the frame and its lock. Offer the ABR-ACTS version; the gear on the truck stays the same.

“It's expensive.”

Count the whole chain: drivers and diesel saved on the long hauls, no terminal, lower emissions (ESG reporting), and the container serves for years. ACTS is an investment in logistics, not a purchase of steel plate.

“What about material blowing off while the train runs?”

On the railway, covering the load is a standard requirement — offer a net, a tarpaulin, lids or a hydraulic roof (we genuinely supply these, see the ABR-ACTS order with a hydraulic roof).

“Who guarantees it will fit the wagon?”

The ABR-ACTS type is built specifically for the swivel frames of the ACTS system. We always put the wagon operator and the frame type into the brief — compatibility is part of the technical specification, not a matter of luck.

Safety and operation

The railway forgives less than the road. Everyone who talks to a customer about ACTS has to know these principles.

Before the train departs

  • The swivel frame in the longitudinal position and locked; the container secured on the frame.
  • The load covered (net/tarpaulin/lids/roof) and distributed so that it stays within the gauge.
  • Doors and flaps closed and secured — nothing may open by itself on the railway.

Ongoing care of the container

  • Check the hook eye and the rollers — wear or deformation = the container is out of service until repaired.
  • Check the frame, floor and welds after impacts; a deformed container will not sit on the frame.
  • Legible marking (owner, type, capacity, tare) and regular inspections under the operator's rules.
Never: do not force the frame round without releasing it, do not leave a frame swung out unattended, do not pull a container across with the doors open, and never let a container with a damaged eye or rollers onto the railway. A single defect stops a whole train.

The questions you must ask before quoting

Without this information you must not confirm the payload, the full usable volume, rail compatibility or ADR/RID fitness. Print it out as a checklist.

Material and weight

  • the exact name of the material plus a photograph of the fraction
  • minimum, average and maximum bulk density
  • the weight of one planned load
  • the required volume and the way it is filled
  • corrosive and abrasive properties
  • how often it is carried and tipped

Equipment and compatibility

  • the method of emptying (doors / flap / open)
  • the type of road gear — hook or chain
  • the type of railway wagon and swivel frame
  • the maximum gross weight on the particular route
  • requirements for sealing, covering and securing

Legislation and operation

  • the UN number and ADR/RID regime (where relevant)
  • the required tests, marking and documentation
  • the country of operation and the railway undertaking
The outcome: only with complete answers can you put together the full ABR-ACTS-… code, confirm the weights and promise compatibility. On the railway, a quotation made from the desk without this data is a gamble.

Worked situations

Try answering for yourself first, then open the recommended solution.

A waste collection firm hauls municipal waste 180 km to an energy-from-waste plant with three trucks a day and is struggling to find drivers.
Recommendation: the classic ACTS case. Move the long haul onto the railway (a wagon with swivel frames = 3 containers) and keep the hooklift trucks only for the “last kilometres” at loading and unloading. Offer tall ABR-ACTS containers with covering (a net or a hydraulic roof) and doors or a flap depending on the plant's technology. The argument: fewer long-distance runs = fewer drivers, less diesel, fewer tolls and lower emissions.
A scrapyard wants “some roll-offs for the train” and insists the ones already on its trucks will do.
Recommendation: explain the difference: an ordinary road roll-off has neither the railway reinforcement nor guaranteed compatibility with the swivel frame. For rail, offer ABR-ACTS in a lower, reinforced version with wear-resistant plate (scrap is abrasive and dense — capacity decides, not volume). Their existing hooklift gear on the trucks stays exactly as it is.
A heating plant is asking about carrying wood chips by rail and is worried about material blowing off and about rain.
Recommendation: wood chips are light and bulky → tall ABR-ACTS containers (large volume) with covering against wind and water: a tarpaulin, lids, or for convenience a hydraulic roof (code SHP/SHL) that the operator works without climbing up. Discharge through the doors or the flap, depending on the plant's hopper.
A customer wants a quotation for “a 7 m ACTS with a hydraulic roof” — what do you need for the brief?
Brief checklist: ① the material carried and its density (this sets the height/volume and the plate), ② exact dimensions and volume, ③ the wagon operator and the swivel frame type (compatibility), ④ the method of emptying (doors/flap/folding front), ⑤ the covering (SHP/SHL roof, tarpaulin, net), ⑥ pull-on gear NH1570 /50 or /60 according to the customer's carrier gear, ⑦ the number of units and the delivery date. The result is a complete ABR-ACTS-… code with nothing left to guesswork.
